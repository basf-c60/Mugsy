Mugsy: annotated disassembly

The economics of Mugsy

This page describes the game's economic model exactly as the VM script at 24652 implements it, what each of your answers actually does, and the strategy that does best. Everything here was worked out from the bytecode and then checked by running a Python reimplementation of the VM (verified to give results identical to the Z80 code) over hundreds of thousands of simulated games.
Conventions: all quantities of money are in thousands of dollars ("grand"). Variables are written with the names used on the variables page: Hoods is the number of hoods, Clients the number of clients, Dough the money in the safe, Blunders the running blunder count (v11), Recruits the hoods recruited, Killed the hoods rubbed out, Kickback the money given to the cops, Ammo the money spent on artillery and ammo, Handout the money paid to the hoods, Loan the loan from Nick the Greek, Price the protection price and Takings the protection takings. Three more names are used only on this page: Random is a random number between 0 and 1, AmmoPerHood is (Ammo / Hoods), and NextTakings and NextPrice are the takings and protection price for next year. Every formula is fully bracketed, with × for multiplication and / for division, so that nothing depends on the order of evaluation. INT means "drop the fractional part". The game's Random is not uniform: values from 0.01 to 0.27 are twice as likely as the others, so its average is 0.42 rather than 0.5.

The year, step by step

Each year the script runs through the following steps in this order. The questions you are asked are in bold.
# Step What happens
1 Report Louey reports last year's losses and recruits. If the feds flag was set last year (see step 16: the flag is one of the VM's flag bytes, at 33888, and is 1 normally or 2 when the feds have been tipped off) and you recruited at least one hood, some recruits were undercover agents: INT((Recruits / 2) + ((10 × Random × Recruits) / Hoods)) hoods die in a shootout, and the flag goes back to 1. The mob is then described as "loyal"; the word "scared" is only used if the flag is still 2, which can only happen when there were no recruits at all.
2 Mob check If the mob has 9 or fewer hoods, they walk out: game over.
3 Takings Takings = INT((Squeezed × Price) + 0.5), where Squeezed is the number of clients squeezed last year. The price was set at the end of last year: (1.5 + (3.5 × Random)), average 2.98 grand per client. If Rocco was "uppity" last year and was not paid off, his mob takes half (when the takings exceed 3).
4 Police raid If a raid was set up last year (see step 15) the cops take that amount, but always leave at least 10 grand in the safe.
5 Nick the Greek If you borrowed last year, the loan plus interest is repaid now. If you cannot pay, Nick takes over (game over).
6 Ammo check If last year's ammo was inadequate (and was not forgiven in step 13, which happens about 61% of the time), cousin Abe muscles in: game over. Never happens in year 1.
7 Blunders The running blunder count is added to the total. If it is more than 2 it is reset to 0, the boys start talking about a contract, and you are asked how many grand to pay as a handout. The hit man will come at the end of the year whatever you pay (see step 13).
8 Rocco Rocco gets uppity if (6 × Random) + 1 is more than 5. That is meant to be a 1 in 3 chance, but because the random routine is uneven it happens about 27% of the time (68 of the 256 raw random values). If your safe holds more than ((2 × Hoods) + Clients + Loan + 100) grand you are asked how much to pay him: any amount of at least 1 grand (leaving more than 5) buys him off. If you are not asked, or pay nothing, he will take half of next year's takings.
9 Clients The price of a client is INT(11 + (10 × Random)) in the first year (11-21 grand) and INT(13 + (6 × Random)) after that (13-19). You are asked how many to buy; if none, how many to sell. You must keep at least as many clients as hoods. If this leaves the safe empty: game over.
10 Ammo You are asked how many grand to spend on artillery and ammo. Less than half a grand per hood (or nothing) is "inadequate" (see step 6). Because of a bug every answer counts as a blunder (see Every ammo answer is a blunder).
11 Squeeze (Clients − Hoods) clients are available (each hood keeps one for himself). You are asked how many to squeeze. Each costs 1 grand now and pays the protection price next year. Squeezing a third or fewer is a blunder. Asking for more than you can afford makes Louey arrange a loan from Nick the Greek for the shortfall (20-799 grand) at INT(40 + (60 × Random))% interest, also a blunder. Asking for (40 × Hoods) or more triggers "the boys aint gonna like workin' that hard": (50 × Hoods) clients are squeezed and the difference is added to (or taken from) the ammo.
12 Kickback You are asked how much to give the cops as a kickback.
13 Hit man If there is a contract, the arcade shootout is played. Mugsy can take between 1 and 8 hits, decided by the handout (see below); the hit man always takes 4. If he wins, game over; if Mugsy wins but bystanders are hit, the cops want INT((20 + (40 × Random))% of the safe). Then, if the ammo was inadequate, it may be forgiven (the inadequate-ammo flag is reset) if a random number is above 0.50. That looks like a coin toss, but because the random routine is uneven it happens only about 39% of the time (100 of the 256 raw random values); otherwise it stays set, which doubles this year's recruits (step 16) but also ends the game at next year's step 6.
14 Next year's price The protection price for next year's takings is set to (1.5 + (3.5 × Random)).
15 Next year's raid Raided = INT(((Dough²) / (Dough + Kickback + 1)) − (Random × Kickback) + ((NextTakings − (8 × Kickback)) / (10 − NextPrice − Blunders))), where Dough is the dough left after the kickback. There is no raid unless this comes to more than 20.
16 Recruits Recruits = INT((((√(Ammo + Kickback)) × Clients) / (Hoods1.5)) − (6 × Blunders)), doubled if the ammo was inadequate and not forgiven (see step 13), limited to 0-99. The doubling is a dead end: the flag that triggers it is still set when next year's report reaches step 6, so cousin Abe ends the game straight away, and that check comes before the end-of-game test, so even a last year is not exempt. The doubled recruits appear in that report and are then never used. The feds flag for next year's report is also set here: it becomes 2 if a random number is 0.80 or more (a 16% chance: 42 of the 256 raw random values) and is 1 otherwise.
17 Losses Killed = INT(((Hoods²) / ((2 × Ammo) + 1)) + (10 × Random × Blunders) − ((20 × Kickback) / (Dough + 1))), limited to 0-99. If that would wipe out the whole mob, Killed = INT(((Blunders + 6) / (Random + Blunders + 5)) × (Hoods + 1)) instead, halved if the ammo was at least 6 grand per hood. Then Hoods = ((Hoods − Killed) + Recruits).

What your answers really do

Squeeze. A squeezed client costs 1 grand and returns 1.5-5 grand (2.98 on average) next year, so squeezing is always profitable. Squeeze every available client you can afford. The "working too hard" rule at 40 hoods' worth can even give you up to a quarter more squeezes for nothing, at the cost of some ammo.
Ammo. Losses are roughly (Hoods / (2 × AmmoPerHood)) a year when you spend AmmoPerHood grand per hood, so 1 grand per hood loses half the mob, 2 grand per hood a quarter, and so on. Ammo also feeds recruitment through (√(Ammo + Kickback)). Below half a grand per hood you risk cousin Abe. Whatever you answer, Louey counts it as a blunder.
Kickback. This is the most powerful lever in the game. In the losses formula it subtracts ((20 × Kickback) / (Dough + 1)), where Dough is what is left in the safe after the kickback, so paying away most of the safe wipes out the year's losses entirely. In the raid formula it cuts the raid three ways (the (Dough²) / (Dough + Kickback + 1) term, the −(Random × Kickback) term and the −(8 × Kickback) term). It also boosts recruitment.
Money in the safe is worth nothing at the end: the final score does not look at it at all. Worse, a full safe attracts raids (the Dough² term means the cops take most of an unprotected safe) and offers no protection against losses. Spend it.
Buying clients costs 13-19 grand. A squeezed client nets about 2 grand a year, so a purchase pays for itself in seven or eight years, but clients also feed recruitment (Clients / (Hoods1.5)) and three of the score terms. Selling is never worthwhile.
Rocco. If you are asked, pay him exactly 1 grand: any amount buys him off.
The handout does not keep the boys faithful at all: the hit man comes regardless. What it does, through a quirk of the fixed-point arithmetic, is set Mugsy's hit points for the shootout: (low 3 bits of ((100 × ((Handout / Hoods) / 3)) + 100), in hundredths) + 1, or 8 if (Handout / Hoods) ≥ 4.68. Paying nothing gives Mugsy 5 hit points, and each extra grand moves the value up one step, so the useful amounts are the small ones that land on 8; paying a little more wraps round to 1, which is the worst result. The hit man always has 4. (The code settles which is which: the keys move the figure whose death is reported as "Mugsy killed", and that figure is the one whose hit points the handout sets.)

The 25-hoods row is not a misprint. Of every mob size from 10 to 200 it is the only one where no small handout gives 8 hit points: (100 × ((Handout / 25) / 3)) works out to 4/3 of the handout, rounded down, and that sequence steps over the one value that would land on 8. Only the "4.68 grand per hood or more" rule gets there, which for 25 hoods means 117 grand.
Loans from Nick the Greek are a blunder, cost 40-100% interest and can end the game, and the search below found no strategy that benefits from them.

How the score works

The final score (24652, from 26469) is the sum of these terms, capped at 100 and never less than 0. Money in the safe plays no part. The mob, clients and profit terms are floored at the constants −0.05 and −0.10 rather than at 0, so a shrinking mob or client list costs a small fraction of a point instead of nothing; this looks like a slip, since every other floor and ceiling is a whole number.
Term How it is worked out Range
Base Always (added as 10 and 35) 45
Surviving Only if the game ended on time (see The game almost never ends on time) 0 or 10
Years (−3) × (Years − Year), at most −12 but with no upper limit: every year beyond Years (v36) adds 3, where Year is the year reached −12 upwards
Mob INT(HoodGrowth / 10), where HoodGrowth = (((Hoods − 40) × 100) / 40) −0.05 to 15
Takings INT(2 × ((TotalTakings / HoodYears) − 25)) 0 to 10
Clients INT(ClientGrowth / 4), where ClientGrowth = (((Clients − StartClients) × 100) / StartClients) −0.10 to 17
Profit INT(20 × ((TotalProfit / ClientYears) − 2.5)) −0.10 to 10
Blunders −TotalBlunders unlimited
Killed −INT(TotalKilled / 4) 0 to −20
Two things dominate. Because every ammo answer is a blunder and the running count is added to the total every year, blunders cost about 2 points a year whatever you do. And because the game almost never ends on time, every year you survive beyond Years is worth 3 points. So a long game gains about 1 point a year once the other terms have settled. In practice the "killed" term hits its −20 limit within a few years, the clients term reaches its maximum of 17 once you have about 670 clients, and the mob and profit terms contribute little: with an average protection price of 2.98 grand, a profit of 3 grand per client-year is out of reach.
Note that dying does not cost anything beyond ending the game: the score is worked out in exactly the same way whether Mugsy is shot, his mob walks out or you lose to Nick the Greek. The only way to end the game on purpose is to lose a shootout, and the hit man comes every third year (sooner if you make extra blunders), so once your score is where you want it, simply stand still the next time he arrives.

The best strategy

To find the best strategy, a parameterised player (ammo per hood, target clients per hood, share of spare cash spent on clients, share of available clients squeezed, share of the remaining safe paid as a kickback, cash kept back, use of loans) was optimised by random search and then coordinate-by-coordinate refinement, scoring each candidate on the same few hundred simulated games and testing the winner on 400 fresh ones. The handout was always the cheapest amount that gives Mugsy 8 hit points (it makes no difference to the score) and Rocco always got 1 grand. Unless stated otherwise the player is assumed to win every shootout.

In words, for a game of about 20 years:
  • Handout: the smallest amount from the table above that gives Mugsy 8 hit points (about 0.1 grand per hood).
  • Rocco: 1 grand.
  • Buy clients: work out what this year will cost (1.5 grand per hood for ammo plus 1 grand per available client for the squeeze, plus 5), and spend about 60% of the rest on clients, until you have about 30-40 clients per hood. Spend more (about 75%) if you plan a short game and less (about 30%) if you plan a very long one.
  • Sell: never.
  • Ammo: 1.5 grand per hood, rounded up.
  • Squeeze: every available client.
  • Kickback: about 45% of what is left, keeping nothing back for next year.
  • Never let ammo + kickback come to 156, 1407, 2499, 2501, 3905 or one of the larger values listed under Some numbers freeze the game (the game freezes), and never borrow from Nick.

Borrowing from Nick the Greek whenever possible lowered the score from 54.6 to 51.6, and deliberately squeezing 40 hoods' worth or more (to get the 50-hood allowance) made no difference.
The reasons:
  • Too little ammo lets losses ((Hoods²) / ((2 × Ammo) + 1)) outrun recruitment and the mob walks out; too much costs more than the hoods are worth, starving the squeeze and the kickback, and the mob collapses the same way.
  • Too small a kickback leaves losses and raids unchecked; too large a kickback triggers the INT sign bug, so the cops raid you anyway and take next year's takings.
  • Cash kept in the safe both invites raids (the Dough² term) and weakens the kickback's protection against losses (−((20 × Kickback) / (Dough + 1))).
  • Buying clients drives the clients term (up to 17 points), the takings and recruitment (which grows with clients per hood); spending everything on them leaves too little for ammo and the kickback.
  • The shootout comes every third year, so even a 90% success rate means that half of all games end before year 20, which is why the 8-hit-point handout matters.
The Python reimplementation of the VM, the strategy code and the optimiser are included with this disassembly (in the tools folder: pyvm.py, policy.py, optimise.py, refine.py, sensitivity.py). The VM was checked against the Z80 code by recording the random numbers and answers from long simulated games and replaying them: every variable and flag matched.
A caveat: all the figures on this page assume the questions are answered sensibly. The client-buying question can be made to overflow and pay you millions of grand (see Buying clients: the cost can overflow and pay you); since money does not enter the score and the score terms are capped, this does not appear to help.
